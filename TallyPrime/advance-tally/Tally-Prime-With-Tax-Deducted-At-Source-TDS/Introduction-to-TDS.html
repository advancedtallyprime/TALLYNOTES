1. Core Concept of TDS

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a direct taxation mechanism introduced by the Government of India under the Income Tax Act, 1961. Under this system, any person or company making specified payments (such as salary, rent, commission, professional fees, contractor payments) is statutorily required to deduct a fixed percentage of tax at the source before paying the recipient, and remit that deducted tax to the Central Government.

2. Key Statutory Terminology

Term Definition Example
Deductor The business entity or individual making the payment and responsible for deducting tax. National Trading Co. paying office rent to landlord.
Deductee The recipient of the income from whom tax is deducted. The landlord or professional consultant receiving fees.
TAN Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number — a 10-digit alphanumeric identifier mandated for all deductors. BLRN12345F
PAN Permanent Account Number of the deductee required to verify tax deduction at standard rates. ABCDE1234F

3. Objectives of TDS Mechanism

  • Accelerated Revenue Collection: Provides steady monthly cash flows to the Government throughout the year.
  • Tax Evasion Prevention: Prevents non-disclosure of income by collecting tax at the very inception of the transaction.
  • Widening the Tax Base: Leaves a permanent paper trail linked to the recipient's PAN.

4. Interview Questions & Answers

Q1. Who is responsible for deducting and depositing TDS?

Ans: The Deductor (payer) is legally responsible for deducting tax at source, depositing it to the government exchequer, filing quarterly TDS returns, and issuing Form 16 / 16A certificates.

Q2. What is TAN and why is it mandatory?

Ans: TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) is a 10-digit alphanumeric code issued by the Income Tax Department. Quoting TAN is legally mandatory on all TDS payments, challans, and quarterly return filings.
